One grill. One range. A full room.
Which ticket do you fire next?

Every plate at is cooked to order. Get the sequence right and the night flows; get it wrong and the pass jams. We hand that one decision — what to cook next — to an AI that writes its own scheduling rule and keeps sharpening it.

The goal

total lateness → 0

For every table, count the minutes its food lands past the promised time, and add them up. Early counts for nothing. A perfect night is zero.

lateness = Σ max(0, ready − promised)  over every table

What's fixed

We don't touch the kitchen, the recipes, or the concept — stays exactly what it is. The only lever is sequence: the order we cook things in. This is the line we have to work with —

each is one cook or pan working at once — the fewer, the tighter

Why it's hard

The bottleneck won't sit still. One table fires two steaks and a burger — three cuts, one grill — while pasta stacks up on the range. Protect the grill and the range falls behind; protect the range and the grill does. No fixed rule wins every night: the right move depends on which station is the wall tonight.

When it goes wrong

Get the sequence wrong and it shows. Here are four nights an early, careless rule mis-scheduled — work piling up behind the bottleneck, every dashed line a deadline blown, the lateness running into the dozens.

How the AI finds the rule

Each round keeps only what beats the best so far, and builds the next rule on it — climbing toward one that holds up on every night, not just an easy one.

The proving ground

Each candidate faces a battery of service nights, every one leaning on a different station — so a winning rule has to be good all round, not just on the easy nights.

Two more nights stay sealed. The kitchen never trains on them, so they're the exam that proves a rule generalised rather than memorised.

What you get

A single priority rule, in plain English — one line the expediter could read off a card at the pass (something like “start whatever's blocking the busiest station”). You can watch it get discovered on the dashboard, see each candidate's schedule on the grid, and trace the winning idea's ancestry on the lineage page.
